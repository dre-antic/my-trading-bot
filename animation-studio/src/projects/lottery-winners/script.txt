Picture winning the lottery. Millions of dollars, overnight. Now picture losing all of it.

You have probably heard that seventy percent of lottery winners go broke within a few years. It is one of the most repeated money facts on the internet. There is just one problem. Nobody can find the study. The group it is usually credited to, the National Endowment for Financial Education, says the number did not come from their research.

So what really happens when ordinary people get rich overnight?

In Florida, economists followed thousands of lottery winners. They compared people who won big prizes, up to one hundred fifty thousand dollars, with people who won less than ten thousand. In the first two years, the big winners were less likely to go bankrupt. That makes sense. But five years later, both groups had filed for bankruptcy at almost the same rate. The money did not fix the problem. It only delayed it.

Why? Because a windfall does not change your habits. If you spend more than you earn, a bigger pile just takes longer to burn. A new car. A bigger house. A loan to a friend. Each one feels tiny next to the jackpot. Together, they are not tiny at all.

But here is the twist. In Sweden, researchers tracked lottery winners for more than ten years. A decade later, the winners were still more satisfied with their lives. They did not feel much happier from day to day. But when they stepped back and judged their life as a whole, the money clearly helped.

So money is not a curse. It is an amplifier. Good habits get bigger. Bad habits get bigger too.

And honestly, your odds of hitting the Powerball jackpot are about one in two hundred ninety two million. You are far more likely to build wealth the slow way. Spend less than you earn. Invest the difference. Repeat for years. It is boring. It also works.

So the next time someone tells you lottery winners always go broke, ask them one question. Where is the source?
