For years, one number claimed to tell you how much money you need to be happy. Seventy five thousand dollars a year. Earn less, and more money helped. Earn more, and your happiness supposedly flat lined. Congratulations. Your feelings had a salary cap. Then another scientist checked the data, and his results said the opposite. So the two rivals teamed up. What they found was surprising. The real question was not how much money you make. It was which kind of happiness you measure. How you feel day to day, versus how you rate your life when you step back.
