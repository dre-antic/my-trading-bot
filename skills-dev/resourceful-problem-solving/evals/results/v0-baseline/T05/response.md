You can get a same-morning “she isn’t up” warning for about $100. A sensor that knows she has fallen anywhere in the house, with no camera and nothing worn, is a different problem and generally costs more. The useful buy is a small door sensor that tells your phone her morning has started, plus an alarm on your phone for the morning that signal never comes.

## What to buy

This needs ordinary home Wi-Fi at her house, on the 2.4 GHz band. She does not need a phone. The app goes on yours. You install it on one visit.

| Piece | What it does for you | Listed price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Aqara Hub E1](https://us.aqara.com/products/hub-e1) | Plugs into a USB charger near her router and links the sensors to the internet | $29.99 |
| [Aqara door and window sensor](https://us.aqara.com/products/door-and-window-sensor) on the bedroom door | Sends a phone notification when that door opens | $17.99 |
| [Aqara Motion Sensor P1](https://us.aqara.com/products/motion-sensor-p1) in the kitchen or the hall | Shows whether she made it into the rest of the house later | $22.99 |

Those are Aqara’s own US store prices, before tax and shipping. Check them again before you order.

Put the door sensor on the door she opens when she gets up. Aim the motion sensor across the path she walks to make breakfast, away from a window, a heater, or a pet’s spot. In the Aqara Home app, turn on notifications for the bedroom door. Then put a repeating alarm on your phone for 30–60 minutes after she is usually up, labeled “Mom up?”

- The door notification is already there: she is up. Dismiss the alarm.
- It is not there: call the phone she actually answers. If she does not pick up, call someone who can reach her door in minutes.

That local person is part of the setup. You are three hours away, so an alert only helps if a neighbor, friend, or relative has a key and has agreed to walk over. Put their number in the alarm label.

Keep a short daily call as well, on whatever phone she already picks up. The sensor covers the morning she cannot get to that phone.

## Tell her what it is

She has already refused a pendant and cameras, so install this only with a yes. The door sensor is two small pieces and a magnet, with no lens. The plain version: when the bedroom door opens, your phone gets a note, and if the note does not come you call her. It does not record sound or pictures. She can pull it off the door. If she says no, leave it, keep the daily call, and ask someone nearby to notice ordinary signs such as curtains or a picked-up paper.

## What you will actually learn

A missing morning notification means “call now.” The cause might be that she has not gotten up, that she is up and unwell and has not opened that door, or that the Wi-Fi or power is out. Any of those is worth a call.

The kitchen sensor is the daytime check. If you look at lunchtime and it has been still since the morning ping, call again. A fall at 10 a.m. shows up only as “the house went quiet.” These sensors see movement. A person lying still on the floor produces the same reading as an empty room, and only after you notice the gap.

Before you drive home, open the bedroom door and confirm your phone buzzes, walk through the kitchen and confirm that one shows up, and unplug the router for a minute so you know what a dead internet connection looks like. Glance at battery status in the app every couple of months.

A dog or cat will trip the motion sensor. In that case use the bedroom door only.

## If the specific fear is a fall beside the bed

The [Aqara Presence Sensor FP2](https://www.amazon.com/Aqara-Positioning-Multi-Person-Detection-Assistant/dp/B0BXWZMQJ3) is the one product near this budget that tries to recognize a fall with radar, so it does not take a picture and she wears nothing. It was listed around $80. It connects to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi on its own, with no hub, and it needs constant USB power at the spot where you mount it.

Fall detection works with it on the ceiling, over a small circle. A November 2026 review cites Aqara’s manual at about a 6.5-foot radius from a 9-foot ceiling. In that mode the other features switch off, the alert goes to the Aqara app only, and Aqara says it is not a medical device. One unit watches the patch of floor under it. A fall in the hall or the kitchen never reaches it. The cord has to reach the ceiling without crossing where she walks.

I would set up the door sensor first. Add the FP2 only if she will accept one ceiling unit in one room and you can power it safely. It is an extra layer, and it is not a medical alert service. Nobody at a call center is sitting behind it.

Whole-room products that send a stick-figure clip, such as the AltumView Sentinare, still use an optical sensor. Given how she feels about cameras, I would leave that category alone.

## Products aimed at a different situation

Bed-exit pads and bedside floor mats are built for a caregiver in the same house. They alarm when she gets up, often loudly in the bedroom, and a mat on the floor is another thing to trip over. They do not tell you she never got up. Professional monitoring (a person who answers at 3 a.m.) is a monthly fee and usually still expects a button or a much pricier room sensor. Worth asking about later if you want someone other than you to act on an alert. It does not fit a one-time $100 cap.

## The two facts that change this

Does she have working home internet, and is there anyone local with a key? Without Wi-Fi, these sensors cannot reach your phone, and the plan becomes the daily call plus that neighbor. A cellular medical-alert base is the other route when there is no internet; it is a subscription, and many of them still want something worn.

If you say whether she has Wi-Fi, a nearby person, a pet, and a ceiling you could get power to beside the bed, the list gets down to a single order.
