## What you are opening

1. Three candles people can buy once.
2. One club: one full-size candle a month, a printed scent card, and a small pack of matches, in a box.

## Do it in this order

### 1. Make the account

1. Go to [shopify.com/pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) and start the Basic plan.
2. Use a dedicated email, something like `hello@` an address you check daily. You will get order mail there.
3. Store name: `[Shop Name]`.
4. When it asks what you sell, say physical products you make yourself.
5. Leave the address as the free `something.myshopify.com` link for now. Buy a domain only after you like the store. A domain is about the price of one candle per year, and you can connect it later under Settings → Domains.

### 2. Make the store look like a candle shop

1. Online Store → Themes. Keep the free theme Shopify gives you. Skip paid themes.
2. Theme editor: set the colors to warm and quiet. Cream background, black text, one brown or amber button color. Use the same colors on the label if you can.
3. Homepage, top to bottom:
   - A large photo of one of your real candles.
   - The headline and buttons in the paste section below.
   - The three candles.
   - The club.
   - A short “how it’s made” note.
4. Add your real photos only. Take them by a window, phone steady, same table or cloth every time. For each candle: the jar straight on, the jar from above so the wax shows, the label close up, and the jar in someone’s hand so the size is obvious. A lit photo is optional and never the only photo. Trim the wick, sit it on a plate, and keep it away from curtains.

### 3. Turn on money, tax, and shipping

1. Settings → Payments → complete Shopify Payments. Have your bank account ready. Use your own legal name and details.
2. Settings → Taxes. Turn on Shopify Tax so checkout can add sales tax. Candles are taxable in most states. You still register for a sales-tax permit with your state when your state requires it. Search “[your state] sales tax permit” on your state tax department’s site.
3. Settings → Customer accounts → turn accounts on. People need an account to skip, pause, or cancel the club. Write that on the club page so it feels like a feature.
4. Settings → Shipping and delivery.
   - On the general profile, United States, add one rate named `US shipping` for **$7**. Delete extra rates so you don’t accidentally offer free shipping on single candles.
   - Create a second shipping profile named `Candle Club`. Put only the club product in it. Give the United States one rate named `Shipping included` at **$0**.

Renewal orders from the free subscription app can fall back to the cheapest shipping rate. The club profile stays at $0, so a subscriber is not surprised by a new postage charge.

### 4. Add the three candles

Products → Add product. For each scent, paste the description further down and fill the blanks.

- Price: **$28**
- Charge shipping (the $7 rate). Do not check “this is a physical product” off. It is physical.
- Inventory: track it. Put the number you have poured and cured, not the number you hope to pour this weekend.
- Weight: weigh the packed box and enter that, so labels are closer to right.

### 5. Add the club

Create one product named `[Shop Name] Monthly Candle Club`.

- Price: **$32**
- Description: the club text below.
- Inventory: don’t track a quantity on this product if Shopify will let you sell it as an ongoing offer. You are pouring to the subscriber count. If the theme forces a quantity, set it high and lower it whenever you are two weeks from a ship date and you are out of jars.
- Then install Shopify Subscriptions from the admin search box. Open the club product and add one plan: deliver every 1 month, no extra discount (the $32 price is already the club price). Leave weekly and yearly off.

On the club page, say clearly: skip, pause, or cancel from the account page any time before the next charge.

### 6. Paste the policies

Settings → Policies. Shopify has starter text for shipping, returns, privacy, and terms. Use those, then replace the shipping and returns parts with the short versions below. Those starters are there so you are not publishing a shop with blank policy links.

### 7. Place one order yourself

Before you send the link to anyone:

1. Buy one candle with your own card.
2. Buy the club with your own card.
3. Open the customer account and cancel the club. You want to see that the button exists.
4. Refund both orders (Orders → Refund).
5. Read the emails that arrived. If a sentence is wrong, fix the product, then look again.

### 8. Tell people it exists

A new Shopify shop has no visitors of its own. Send the link to people who already like your candles.

- Text and email 20 people who have smelled them. Ask them to tell you which photo made the size clear.
- Post the same three photos on Instagram or Facebook, with the shop link, once a week.
- Put the link in your phone’s Instagram bio.
- If you sell at a market, print the shop link as a QR code on one card.

Leave the club unpublished until you have poured the next two months of candles and you have shipped at least a few single candles on time. In Shopify, set the club product to draft, then set it active on the day you are ready. The single-candle shop can go live this weekend.

### The jar label

This is a checklist, not legal advice. US candle labels sit under the Fair Packaging and Labeling Act (16 CFR Part 500) and the CPSC’s lead-wick rule. Fire-safety wording follows the industry practice in ASTM F2058. Read these before you print a stack of labels:

- [CPSC candle guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Candles)
- [National Candle Association on labels and safety statements](https://candles.org/candle-labels-101-understanding-product-claims-safety-instructions/)
- [16 CFR Part 500](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-500)

On the front of the label:

- What it is: `Scented candle`
- Your shop name
- Your city and state
- Net weight of the **wax only**, in ounces and grams, near the bottom. Example shape: `Net Wt 8 oz (227 g)`. Weigh your wax. The jar does not count. Use weight, not fluid ounces.

On the back, or on the bottom, a safety block in this spirit:

`WARNING: To prevent fire, burn within sight. Keep away from things that can catch fire. Keep away from children and pets. Trim wick to 1/4 inch before lighting. Stop burning when 1/2 inch of wax remains.`

Also:

- Buy wicks whose seller states they are lead-free. Lead-cored wicks are banned in the US. Cotton and wood wicks are the normal choice. If you ever use a metal-core wick, the CPSC limit is 0.06% lead, and that shipment needs the conformity statement on the outer shipping package.
- Ask your fragrance supplier whether any warning applies to your oils, including California Proposition 65 if you ship to California.
- Skip health claims. “Cleans the air,” “aromatherapy cure,” and “non-toxic” are claims you would have to prove. “Phthalate-free” only if the supplier’s sheet says so.
- Cure soy candles the number of days your wax maker recommends, often about one to two weeks, before you sell them. A candle that was poured yesterday often burns a tunnel in the middle.

## Before the first stranger’s order

- Photograph and list only candles that are already cured and labeled.
- Put product-liability insurance on your list this month. Candles are a fire product, and a homeowner policy often leaves out a home business. A local independent agent can quote a small-maker policy.
- Check your city’s home-business rules and, if you rent, your lease. Candle making is not covered by cottage-food rules, because it is not food.
- Pour the club’s next two scents before you turn the club product on. Members remember a late box more than they remember a pretty photo.
- Ship one box to yourself or a friend. Open it the way a customer will. If the jar touches the cardboard, add more paper.
